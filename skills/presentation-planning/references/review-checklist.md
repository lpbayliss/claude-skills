# Presentation Review Checklist

Use this checklist to review an outline, script, slide plan, or production brief. Lead with high-impact findings and repair the underlying narrative before visual polish.

## Contents

- Communication contract
- Story and reasoning
- Audience fit
- Evidence and technical integrity
- Spoken and visual roles
- Timing and delivery
- Accessibility and production readiness
- Verdict

## Communication contract

- [ ] The occasion, duration, format, and Q&A/interaction constraints are known or labelled.
- [ ] A primary audience and its decision/learning stakes are explicit.
- [ ] The desired audience change is observable, not merely `inform` or `engage`.
- [ ] One governing idea unifies the presentation.
- [ ] The action, decision, or lasting implication is clear where applicable.

## Story and reasoning

- [ ] The opening establishes relevance and tension quickly.
- [ ] The story progresses through necessary questions or claims rather than source-document order.
- [ ] Each beat advances, qualifies, or applies the governing idea.
- [ ] Transitions explain why the next beat follows.
- [ ] The ending resolves the opening and lands the implication before Q&A.
- [ ] Narrative devices serve the idea and do not become emotional decoration.
- [ ] Repetition reinforces the central idea without restating whole sections.

Failure signals:

- agenda as opening;
- several unrelated “key messages”;
- chronology of work rather than logic of insight;
- surprise conclusion withheld from decision-makers;
- forced anecdote or generic rhetorical question;
- recap-only ending;
- ask introduced without evidence.

## Audience fit

- [ ] The opening connects to what this audience values or must decide.
- [ ] Prerequisites are sufficient but not excessive.
- [ ] Jargon, symbols, diagrams, units, and scales match audience fluency.
- [ ] Numbers have meaningful context.
- [ ] Mixed audiences receive a shared model and layered depth.
- [ ] Legitimate objections and competing priorities are represented fairly.
- [ ] Tone matches the relationship and stakes without hype or condescension.

## Evidence and technical integrity

- [ ] Every consequential claim is sourced, accepted, or clearly marked unresolved.
- [ ] Evidence actually supports the strength and scope of the claim.
- [ ] Counter-evidence, uncertainty, and limitations are not hidden.
- [ ] Observed, simulated, projected, illustrative, and planned results are distinct.
- [ ] Causal language is justified.
- [ ] Comparisons share definitions, populations, units, and baselines.
- [ ] Methods and implementation detail are included only where needed for trust or action.
- [ ] The presentation does not invent metrics, quotes, customer stories, approvals, or outcomes.

For high-impact specialist claims—security, safety, medicine, law, causal inference, or complex distributed systems—require domain review before treating the content as final.

## Spoken and visual roles

- [ ] The speaker’s reasoning is not duplicated as dense on-screen prose.
- [ ] Each planned visual has a clear communicative purpose.
- [ ] Each beat has one principal point.
- [ ] Slide titles state takeaways or purposeful questions rather than topic labels.
- [ ] Visual evidence supports the assertion.
- [ ] Complex data/diagrams are introduced before interpretation.
- [ ] Extraneous decoration, repeated branding, and irrelevant detail are absent.
- [ ] Some beats intentionally use no slide where speech is stronger.

## Timing and delivery

- [ ] The plan fits the real talk time with pauses and transitions.
- [ ] Q&A, interaction, and demo variability are budgeted.
- [ ] Core, compressible, cut-first, and backup content are identified.
- [ ] The short version removes beats instead of accelerating delivery.
- [ ] Opening and landing receive enough rehearsal attention.
- [ ] Demo success criterion and fallback are explicit.
- [ ] Likely questions and evidence-backed short answers are prepared.
- [ ] Unknown answers have an honest response and follow-up path.

## Accessibility and production readiness

- [ ] Viewing distance and remote/hybrid conditions shape content density.
- [ ] Planned visuals can use legible type, contrast, and colour-independent encoding.
- [ ] Essential visual information can be described verbally or through accessible notes.
- [ ] Captioning, transcript, language, and interaction needs are addressed.
- [ ] Claims and assets have traceable sources and rights status.
- [ ] A producer knows what to build without inventing the message.
- [ ] A speaker knows what to say without reading the visual layer.
- [ ] Missing assets, approvals, evidence, and production decisions are explicit.

## Verdict

Use one verdict for the named deliverable:

- **Production-ready** — narrative, evidence, timing, and handoff are coherent; physical production may proceed.
- **Conditionally production-ready** — production can begin, with named non-gating follow-up before delivery.
- **Not production-ready** — unresolved governing idea, audience, evidence, timing, or high-impact claim would force the producer/speaker to invent material.

Lead a review with blocking findings, then important improvements, then optional polish. Cite the affected beat, title, section, or source line. Do not rewrite the entire presentation unless requested.
